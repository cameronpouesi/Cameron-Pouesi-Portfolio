# Product

## Platform

web

## Users

Producers, post-production supervisors, series producers and production
managers at New Zealand and international factual/broadcast companies,
deciding whether to book an offline editor for a series, documentary or
campaign. They arrive from a CV, a referral or a link in an application,
usually with a specific slot to fill and limited time. Their job is to
answer two questions fast: has this person cut work like mine, and can I
trust them with an episode.

Brands and agencies booking commercial and branded content are a real
secondary audience, but the site leads with broadcast credits.

## Product Purpose

A portfolio for Cameron Pouesi, offline editor (10+ years, reality
television, documentary, commercials and digital content). It exists to
turn a credit list into evidence — showing the work moving rather than
listing it — and to convert that into an email enquiry.

Success is an email. Watching is how the case gets built, but the site is
sequenced so that being impressed leads to getting in touch.

## Positioning

Most editor portfolios are a grid of thumbnails and a CV. This one is an
interactive exhibition: each category is a believable physical space —
a stack of CRTs in a warehouse, an archive of 35mm negatives, an edit
suite, a paste-up wall — that the visitor moves through, with the work
playing inside the objects. The craft of the site is itself the argument
for the craft of the editing.

## Operating Context

Evaluated in a browser, usually desktop, often between other tasks and
sometimes with sound off. Visitors skim first and commit to watching only
once something catches them. Many will already know the formats by name
(The Traitors, Taskmaster, RuPaul's Drag Race Down Under, Celebrity
Treasure Island), so recognisable key art does a lot of the work.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- React + Vite; React Three Fiber / drei / three for the environments;
  Framer Motion for 2D reveals; postprocessing for the shared film grade.
- Six categories: Reality TV, Documentaries, Comedy, Advertising,
  Children's TV, Freelance. Comedy, Advertising and Children's TV share
  one continuous three-chapter room.
- Every project is defined in `src/data/projects.js`; companies and their
  logos in `src/data/companies.js`. Content changes need no code changes.
- Two video tiers: full clips in `/public/video/clips` for the
  click-through lightbox, short silent-until-hovered loops in
  `/public/video/previews` generated by `make-previews.sh`.
- Hover previews must be local mp4; a YouTube embed cannot be read into a
  WebGL texture, so YouTube-hosted projects show artwork and play in the
  lightbox only.
- Portrait (9:16) work must be shown on portrait-shaped surfaces. Never
  letterboxed into landscape hardware.
- Only one screen anywhere is audible at a time; browsers require a real
  user gesture before any of it can be heard.
- Every section carries a "Full Credit List" that opens the plain
  Project / Company / Broadcaster / Role table for that category. It reads
  the same `projects.js` records the hover cards do — a credit is never
  entered twice, so the two views cannot disagree.

## Brand Commitments

- **Credit and metadata accuracy is binding.** Company, role, broadcaster
  and channel must be exactly right on every project. Never invent,
  approximate or pad a credit to make a card look complete. If a fact
  isn't known, the field is omitted rather than guessed.
- **The work stays the hero.** Environments exist to frame the footage.
  No environment, prop, lighting effect or transition may obscure,
  compete with or crop a thumbnail or preview.
- Reality TV and Documentaries are the two lead sections and carry the
  most weight; Comedy, Advertising, Children's TV and Freelance are
  deliberately lighter.
- Contact details are supplied by Cameron and used verbatim:
  Cameronpouesi@hotmail.co.nz, Instagram `Cameronpouesi`, LinkedIn
  `linkedin.com/in/cameron-pouesi-5703716a/`.
- Third-party imagery is not embedded as site texture; environment
  materials are CC0 (Poly Haven) or generated.

is still placeholder text and no real ones have been supplied. There are
no testimonials, client quotes, awards, metrics or press. The YouTube
channel URL has never been confirmed. Do not invent any of these.

## Product Principles

1. **Evidence over assertion.** Show the cut, don't claim the skill.
2. **Recognition is the hook.** Lead with the formats a hiring producer
   will already know by name.
3. **The room serves the work.** Environments earn their place only by
   making the footage more compelling to look at, never less legible.
4. **Accuracy is non-negotiable.** A wrong credit costs more than an
   empty field.
5. **Impressed should lead somewhere.** Every path ends within reach of
   an email.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Reduced-motion is honoured throughout: scroll-driven cameras, drifting
objects and entrance animations all fall back to static presentation.
Audio never starts unprompted. No product-specific standard beyond this
has been established.
